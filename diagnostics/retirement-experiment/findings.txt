Conclusion: In all 3 Normal trials, all 144 bytes including the 21 original positive token IDs survived Session deletion and engine destruction unchanged. This is direct evidence that those bytes remained recoverable at these checkpoints. They changed only after loading the next model (5,5,0 of 21 original same-position IDs then matched; incidental matches after reuse do not establish ownership).
Ghost consumed positions were already zero before close. After Session deletion the allocation showed other data. This experiment does NOT isolate or prove the transient zero-write performed by ScrubAll before reuse, nor cover pending-ID storage or other token copies.
A mapped/resident virtual page does not establish physical PFN identity or prove that no madvise/unmap/remap occurred between checkpoints. No page-table/PFN access or syscall trace was used. This is not a universal allocator guarantee, especially for larger/mmap-backed KV buffers.
